The short answer

Buy the De'Longhi La Specialista Arte Evo ($699.95) if you want to actually make espresso — grind, dose, tamp, pull — and you want a machine that also does cold coffee properly, with its own dedicated low-temperature extraction cycle. Buy the De'Longhi Magnifica Evo ($869.00) if you want the same cup of coffee every morning at the press of one saved button, with no portafilter, no tamper and almost nothing to clean by hand.

These are not two versions of the same machine at two prices. They are De'Longhi's answer to two different questions. The Arte Evo asks "how much control can we hand a beginner without overwhelming them," and answers it with a built-in grinder, a guided interface and a cold-brew-adjacent mode most semi-automatics in this price range skip entirely. The Magnifica Evo asks "how little does the user need to do," and answers it by owning every step from bean to cup, including the part where it remembers what you drink.

Same brand, two different jobs

Both machines are made by De'Longhi, both grind whole beans, and both sit in the upper-middle of the home espresso market — but the machine class underneath is different, and that single fact explains almost every other difference in this article.

The La Specialista Arte Evo is a semi-automatic with a built-in grinder. You still clamp a portafilter under the group head, you still start and stop the shot, and the machine's job is to make every step up to that point easier and more consistent: grinding on demand at a chosen setting, holding temperature, and offering a cold-extraction cycle for iced drinks instead of just dumping a hot shot over ice.

The Magnifica Evo is a super-automatic. There is no portafilter to clamp, because there is no portafilter — the machine grinds into an internal brewing chamber, tamps it mechanically, brews, ejects the puck, and repeats that sequence every time you press a button. The thing you interact with is a one-touch recipe, not a shot.

Neither approach is a downgrade of the other. A semi-automatic with the Arte Evo's level of grinder control gives you a higher ceiling for dialling in a specific bean. A super-automatic with the Magnifica Evo's saved-recipe system gives you a floor that never drops, no matter who in the house presses the button or how rushed the morning is. The $375 price gap is De'Longhi charging for the second kind of convenience, not simply for "more machine."

What 15 grind settings and a cold-extraction mode buy on the Arte Evo

The Arte Evo's built-in grinder offers 15 grind settings, which is a meaningful range for a machine at this price — enough room to walk from a noticeably coarser setting for a fruity, lighter-roast bean down to a fine setting for a darker roast that chokes easily, without running out of adjustment in either direction. Grind size is the single biggest lever over how a shot tastes, more than the machine itself: too coarse and water races through the puck in a few seconds, sour and thin; too fine and the shot drips for a minute, over-extracted and bitter. Fifteen steps means you are rarely stuck between "a bit sour" and "a bit bitter" with no setting in between.

The other headline spec is the dedicated cold-extraction mode. Most espresso machines treat iced coffee as an afterthought: pull a normal hot shot, pour it over ice, and accept that the ice dilutes and the heat shocks some of the aromatics out of the cup. A cold-extraction cycle instead brews specifically for a cold result — typically at different pressure and flow parameters tuned for serving over ice rather than for a hot cup — which is a feature you otherwise have to buy a separate cold-brew device to get. For anyone whose daily drink is an iced latte more often than a hot cappuccino, that is not a gimmick; it changes which machine actually fits your habit.

None of this comes free of effort. A grinder with 15 settings still requires you to find the right one for your bean, and the brand's own marketing around "cold-extraction" describes a mode, not a guarantee of café-quality cold brew — we have no independent lab data on extraction yield or temperature curves for this unit, so treat the mode as a real capability worth testing in your own kitchen rather than a number to compare against competitors.

What saved recipes and dishwasher-safe parts buy on the Magnifica Evo

The Magnifica Evo's case is built around two things that look small on a spec sheet and matter every single day: saved recipes and dishwasher-safe removable parts.

Saved recipes mean the machine remembers a drink the way you like it — strength, volume, milk if the model's frother is engaged — and reproduces it on a single press, rather than asking you to set strength and volume each time the way a simpler one-touch machine does. In a household where two or three people drink different things, that is the difference between "everyone gets their usual" and "someone has to remember the settings." It is also the clearest expression of the super-automatic philosophy: the decision happens once, at setup, and never again.

Dishwasher-safe parts solve the maintenance problem that super-automatics are usually criticized for. A machine that grinds, tamps and brews internally has more surfaces that touch coffee oils and milk residue than a simple portafilter machine, and if those parts have to be hand-washed and air-dried, upkeep becomes a chore that gets skipped. Being able to pull the relevant components and run them through a dishwasher cycle lowers the actual weekly effort of owning a super-automatic, which is the whole point of buying one in the first place — you are not trying to add a second ritual on top of the one the machine already removed.

The trade is the one every super-automatic makes: no portafilter to pull apart and inspect, no manual tamping to feel whether your dose is right, and a brewing chamber that is somewhat more opaque about what is actually happening to your coffee. You are trusting the machine's internal grind-dose-tamp sequence rather than controlling each step yourself.

Where the $375 gap actually goes

At $699.95 for the Arte Evo and $869.00 for the Magnifica Evo, the price difference is $125.02 on our recorded prices — a gap worth naming precisely rather than rounding, since Amazon pricing on both machines moves and the gap moves with it. It is useful to separate what you are actually paying for from what simply differs.

You are not paying $125 more for "a better machine" in some single-axis sense. You are paying for the super-automatic's additional internal engineering: a mechanical tamping and brewing sequence that replaces the portafilter workflow entirely, plus the saved-recipe system and a milk frother built into the standard package. The Arte Evo, in exchange for being the cheaper machine here, gives you manual control, a wider grind range you choose yourself, and a cold-extraction mode purpose-built for iced coffee that the Magnifica Evo's listing does not claim to have.

Put differently: the gap is not paying for "more coffee machine," it is paying to remove a step. Every step the Magnifica Evo automates — grinding into a chamber instead of a portafilter, tamping mechanically, brewing and ejecting the puck without your involvement — is a step the Arte Evo still asks you to do by hand, with the Arte Evo's 15-setting grinder and cold mode as the compensation for doing that work yourself.

Who should buy the cheaper machine and learn to use the grinder

The Arte Evo is the right buy for someone who is at least a little curious about the mechanics of espresso, even if they are not yet confident in them. You do not need prior experience to use a built-in grinder with 15 settings — you need patience for the first week or two of finding the setting that suits your bean, which is exactly the learning curve our matched machine-and-grinder guide walks through for bundles like this one. If that sounds engaging rather than annoying, the $125.02 you save over the Magnifica Evo buys you a genuinely capable semi-automatic plus a grinder, which is usually the more expensive half of a separate setup.

It is also the better fit if cold coffee is your actual daily habit rather than an occasional summer order. A dedicated cold-extraction cycle is a specific, purpose-built answer to "how do I make this well at home" — something our super-automatic picks generally do not claim to solve, since one-touch machines are built around hot one-touch recipes first.

The Magnifica Evo is the better fit for the opposite kind of person: someone who wants espresso to be a solved problem, not a hobby. If you have already decided you do not want to grind, dose or tamp — and you would rather spend the extra $125.02 making sure cleanup is a dishwasher cycle rather than a sink full of parts — buy the super-automatic and do not feel like you are settling. Our legacy brand comparison covers why an established name like De'Longhi tends to charge a premium for exactly this kind of integrated, low-maintenance engineering, and it is a premium this comparison bears out directly.

One thing not to assume

Do not assume the pricier machine is simply "the better one." The Arte Evo is not a stripped-down Magnifica Evo — it is a different class of machine with its own standout spec the Magnifica Evo does not offer. If cold extraction or manual grinding is what you actually want, the cheaper machine here is the correct answer, not the compromise.

Frequently asked questions

Is the De'Longhi Magnifica Evo just a more expensive version of the Arte Evo?

No. They are different machine classes. The Arte Evo is a semi-automatic — you grind, dose and tamp into a portafilter yourself, with 15 grind settings and a dedicated cold-extraction mode. The Magnifica Evo is a super-automatic that grinds, doses, tamps and brews internally with no portafilter at all, and adds saved one-touch recipes plus dishwasher-safe parts. The $125.02 gap on our recorded prices pays for that automation, not for a generically "better" machine.

What does the Arte Evo's cold-extraction mode actually do?

It brews specifically for serving cold rather than pulling a normal hot shot and pouring it over ice. That typically means different brewing parameters tuned for a cold result. We have no independent lab data on the exact extraction behavior, so treat it as a real, purpose-built capability worth trying rather than a number to compare against other machines.

Can I control grind size and shot strength on the Magnifica Evo the way I can on the Arte Evo?

Not with the same manual control. The Magnifica Evo is a super-automatic: it grinds into an internal chamber and brews behind a one-touch recipe, so your main point of control is the recipe you save, not a portafilter you dial in yourself. The Arte Evo's built-in grinder gives you 15 settings you choose and apply shot by shot.

Does the Magnifica Evo's milk frother work the same way as a manual steam wand?

No — it is a built-in frother integrated into the super-automatic's one-touch workflow, meant to be used without manual steaming technique. A manual steam wand, which the Arte Evo does not include in the specs we have on file, gives more direct control over milk texture but requires practice. If milk-drink control matters more to you than cold extraction, check the current listing for frothing specifics before buying either machine.
