The short answer

If you just want to know whether the Chefman you're looking at is the right one, here's the shape of the range. The CraftBrew at $79.97 is a bare pump machine: a 15-bar pump, a steam wand, a 1.5 liter reservoir, and no grinder. The Crema Deluxe at $332.81 and the Crema Supreme at $299.00 both add a built-in grinder with 30 grind settings, but they spend the extra money on two different things — the Deluxe on a second boiler that lets you pull a shot and froth milk at the same time, the Supreme on an explicit 3 liter tank and a pressure gauge display.

That means the real decision isn't "which Chefman is best" in the abstract. It's whether you ever make two milk drinks back to back — the Deluxe's reason to exist — or whether a bigger tank and a visible pressure reading matter more to you than that, which is the Supreme's case. We'll walk through each machine's specifics, then put the two grinder models head to head, because that's the comparison most shoppers actually need.

The CraftBrew: Chefman's $79.97 floor

The CraftBrew is the plainest machine Chefman sells, and the listing is honest about that: a 15-bar pump, digital controls, a steam wand for lattes and cappuccinos, and a 1.5 liter removable water reservoir, in a stainless-black finish described as compact. There is no grinder and no second boiler — the two features that separate it from the other two machines in this lineup.

The "15-bar" figure is worth a beat of skepticism, and not because it's false. Espresso brews at roughly 9 bar of pressure at the coffee itself; a 15-bar rating describes what the pump can produce at its maximum, not the pressure your shot is actually pulled at. Every real pump machine, including this one, settles to brewing pressure once the portafilter is in place — the number on the box is a ceiling, not a cup-quality score.

What the CraftBrew gets right for $79.97 is the fundamentals: a genuine pump, a steam wand rather than no milk option at all, and digital controls rather than a single dial. That puts it in the territory covered by our look at espresso machines under $80 — below this range, pump machines get hard to find and steam-driven units show up instead, brewing at a fraction of real espresso pressure.

The absence of a grinder is the bigger practical decision than the price tag. Buying the CraftBrew means either committing to pre-ground coffee, which will always underperform in an espresso machine, or budgeting separately for a burr grinder. If you're weighing that trade-off against simply paying more for a machine that grinds for you, that's exactly the question our guide to grinder bundles works through in more depth than we can here.

Why two of the three grind, and one doesn't

Espresso is unusually sensitive to grind size. Water forced through a bed of coffee at pressure finds the path of least resistance, and the size of the grounds decides how hard that path is — too coarse and the shot races through thin and sour, too fine and it chokes and turns bitter. Pre-ground coffee is milled to a single compromise size meant to work across every brewing method, which means it's always a poor fit for espresso specifically, and it stales within days of grinding no matter how it's packaged.

That's why the jump from the CraftBrew to either the Crema Deluxe or the Crema Supreme is the single biggest change in the lineup, bigger than the price difference between the two grinder models themselves. Both the Deluxe and the Supreme list a built-in grinder with 30 grind settings — identical on paper, which means neither has an edge over the other on grind adjustability. Whatever separates them, it isn't how finely you can dial in the grind.

It's the same fork in the road a lot of espresso shoppers hit regardless of brand — De'Longhi sells a comparable split in its own lineup, which we cover in La Specialista Arte Evo versus Magnifica Evo. The specifics differ by maker, but the underlying question is the same one Chefman buyers face here: pay more up front for a machine that grinds for you, or keep the machine cheaper and own the grind decision separately.

With grind settings tied at 30 on both Chefman grinder models, the comparison between the Deluxe and the Supreme comes down entirely to what each one does with its boiler and its tank — which is where the real price gap in this lineup actually lives.

The Crema Deluxe's second boiler

The Crema Deluxe's listing leads with its defining feature: a double boiler, described as letting you "use milk frother and brew espresso simultaneously." That's a specific, mechanical claim worth unpacking, because it's the reason this machine costs what it costs.

A single-boiler espresso machine heats one reservoir of water to one temperature at a time, and brewing and steaming need different temperatures — brew water runs around 200°F, while steam for texturing milk needs to run hotter still. A single-boiler machine has to switch between the two, which means pulling a shot and then waiting for the boiler to reach steam temperature before you can froth, or the reverse. A second boiler removes that wait by keeping a dedicated heat source for steam, so the machine can brew and froth genuinely at the same time rather than in sequence.

Neither the CraftBrew's listing nor the Crema Supreme's mentions a second boiler. By Chefman's own naming, that capability belongs to the Deluxe alone in this lineup. For a household where two people want milk drinks at the same time in the morning, or where a single morning routine includes both a shot and steamed milk and neither party wants to wait on the other, that's a real functional difference — not a marketing flourish.

It's also worth noting what the Deluxe's listing doesn't specify: an exact water tank capacity. Chefman lists its size as "Large" on Amazon without giving a figure the way it does for the CraftBrew's 1.5 liter reservoir or the Crema Supreme's 3 liter tank. If tank size matters to your routine, that's a question to confirm on the live listing rather than something we can state here.

A second boiler also means a second heating element and a more complex internal plumbing layout than a single-boiler design — generally a reason dual-boiler machines run pricier and heavier than otherwise-similar single-boiler ones, independent of any one brand. If pressure-side safety features are part of your research, our guide to automatic pressure relief valves covers what that feature does on machines where it's specified; none of the three Chefman listings here mention one, so we're not asserting any of them have it.

The Crema Supreme's tank and pressure gauge

Where the Deluxe spends its premium on a second boiler, the Crema Supreme spends its money differently: a 3 liter removable water tank, stated explicitly on the listing, and a pressure gauge display that gives you a visual read of brewing pressure rather than a guess. It also carries the same 15-bar pump rating as the CraftBrew, the same built-in grinder with 30 grind settings as the Deluxe, a milk frother, and a steam wand — stainless steel construction throughout.

The 3 liter figure is twice the CraftBrew's stated 1.5 liter reservoir, and it's the largest tank capacity actually specified anywhere in this lineup — the Deluxe's "Large" size designation doesn't give a number to compare against it. For a household that doesn't want to refill the reservoir every day, or that's making several drinks back to back without running dry, that's a concrete, checkable advantage, not a marketing adjective.

The pressure gauge is a smaller thing but a genuinely useful one. Most home machines in this price range give you no feedback on what's happening inside the group head beyond the taste of the shot itself; a gauge turns an invisible variable into something you can glance at and use to diagnose a bad pull — too little resistance and the needle sits low, a channeled or over-packed puck and it spikes. It's a feature usually reserved for machines priced well above $299.00, which makes its presence here worth factoring into the decision even without a second boiler attached to it.

Deluxe vs. Supreme: $332.81 against $299.00

This is the comparison that actually matters once you've decided you want a Chefman that grinds for you, because the CraftBrew is out of the running the moment you want that feature at all. As of our price checks, the Crema Deluxe runs $33.81 more than the Crema Supreme — a modest gap, and one that could easily flip given how often prices move on Amazon, so treat the ordering as current rather than permanent.

What that gap buys isn't "more machine" in a simple sense; it's a different machine for a different routine. The Deluxe's second boiler solves a timing problem: it lets brewing and frothing happen in parallel instead of one waiting on the other. The Supreme's 3 liter tank and pressure gauge solve a capacity-and-feedback problem: more water between refills, and a way to see what the pump is actually doing. Neither upgrade touches the grinder — both machines grind at the same 30 settings, so you aren't trading away grind quality either way.

Chefman isn't trying to be a legacy espresso specialist competing on heritage the way some brands in this category do; it's a direct-to-consumer appliance brand spreading a feature set across price points, which is worth keeping in mind when you compare it against names with a longer specialist pedigree — a distinction we go into in legacy brand or direct-to-consumer. That framing matters here because the Deluxe-versus-Supreme choice isn't "which is the better machine" in some absolute sense; it's which specific problem — simultaneous milk drinks, or tank capacity and pressure feedback — is actually yours.

If neither problem feels pressing and you're mainly choosing on price, the Supreme's current $299.00 is the easier entry point into Chefman's grinder tier. If you know your mornings involve two people wanting milk drinks at once, the Deluxe's $332.81 buys the one feature that actually fixes that, and no amount of tank capacity on the Supreme substitutes for it.

Which model matches which kitchen

A single person making one or two shots a day, with limited counter space and no urgency about milk timing, is the CraftBrew's buyer. It's the cheapest entry into a real pump machine, and if you already own or are willing to add a separate grinder, the lack of a built-in one isn't a loss — it's a deliberate trade for a lower price, covered in more depth in our grinder bundle guide.

A household where two people both want milk drinks on a weekday morning — one person steaming while the other's shot is still pulling, or vice versa — is the Crema Deluxe's case. The whole reason to pay its premium over the Supreme is that it removes the wait between brewing and frothing; if your mornings don't actually involve that overlap, you're paying for a feature you won't use.

A household that wants one machine to grind, brew and froth, doesn't want to refill the tank constantly, and would like a visual check on brewing pressure rather than just taste as feedback, is the Crema Supreme's case — and at $299.00 against the Deluxe's $332.81, it's currently also the cheaper way into Chefman's grinder tier.

Frequently asked questions

Do all three Chefman models have a built-in grinder?

No. The CraftBrew has no grinder at all and takes pre-ground coffee or coffee ground separately. Both the Crema Deluxe and the Crema Supreme have a built-in grinder with 30 grind settings — identical on paper between the two, so grind adjustability isn't what separates them.

What does the Crema Deluxe's second boiler actually do?

It lets the machine brew espresso and steam milk at the same time rather than in sequence. A single-boiler machine has to switch between brew temperature and the hotter steam temperature, which means waiting between pulling a shot and frothing; a second boiler keeps a dedicated heat source for steam so both can happen in parallel. Neither the CraftBrew's nor the Crema Supreme's listing mentions this feature — it's specific to the Deluxe in this lineup.

Is the Crema Supreme's larger tank worth paying for over the CraftBrew?

The Crema Supreme's 3-liter tank is double the CraftBrew's stated 1.5-liter reservoir, which matters if you don't want to refill daily or you're making several drinks in a row — but the Supreme also costs considerably more than the CraftBrew because it adds a built-in grinder and a pressure gauge, not the tank alone. If tank size is your only concern and you're fine grinding separately, that's a narrower upgrade than buying the full Supreme.

Which Chefman espresso machine should I buy?

It depends on what you actually need rather than which machine costs more. Buy the CraftBrew at $79.97 if you want the cheapest real pump machine and don't need a built-in grinder. Buy the Crema Deluxe at $332.81 if your household needs to brew and froth milk at the same time. Buy the Crema Supreme at $299.00 if you want a built-in grinder, a larger tank and a pressure gauge without paying for the Deluxe's simultaneous-brewing feature.
