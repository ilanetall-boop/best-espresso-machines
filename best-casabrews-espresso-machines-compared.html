The short answer

CASABREWS sells four current espresso machines, and the price ladder tells a cleaner story than most brands manage. The 3700 Essential at $99.99 is the stripped-down entry: pump, portafilter, steam wand, no grinder. The CM5418 shows up twice — once at $139.99, once at $149.99 — same base name, $26 apart (we're quoting the two listing prices as we recorded them; the gap you see live may differ slightly), and the difference is worth tracing before you assume the cheaper listing is simply a better deal. The MARENZA at $299.99 is the outlier: it adds a built-in grinder, an LCD display and a 20-bar pump, and at that point you're no longer buying a CASABREWS entry machine, you're buying a bean-to-cup machine that happens to carry the CASABREWS name.

If you want the one-line version: buy the 3700 Essential if $100 is your ceiling and you already own or plan to buy a grinder. Buy a CM5418 if you want a slightly larger machine for daily milk drinks and are prepared to read the listing carefully. Buy the MARENZA if you want grinder and machine in one box and $300 is within budget.

The 3700 Essential: what $99.99 buys, and what it skips

The CASABREWS 3700 Essential Stainless Steel Espresso Machine with Steam Wand is the floor of the lineup at $99.99, and what's notable is how much of a "real" espresso machine survives at that price: a stainless-steel body, a portafilter instead of a pod slot, and a steam wand instead of an automatic frother you can't adjust. At 12.6 by 5.7 by 12.2 inches it's also the narrowest machine CASABREWS sells, which is a genuine advantage on a tight counter rather than a marketing footnote.

What it skips is the single most expensive component in any espresso setup: a grinder. There is no bean hopper, no burr assembly, nothing between the bag and the portafilter. That's not a flaw specific to this machine — no espresso machine under roughly $150 in any brand's lineup includes a grinder worth using — but it does mean the $99.99 sticker isn't the full cost of a working setup. Budget for pre-ground espresso roast, or for a separate burr grinder if you want to dial in grind size, which is the single biggest lever over how a shot tastes.

The other thing a machine this size and price skips is boiler capacity. We don't have a published boiler-size figure for the 3700 Essential to quote here, but a compact 12.2-inch-tall steam-driven unit in this class is built around a single small heating element, which is why back-to-back milk drinks on entry machines generally mean a short wait between the first and second steam cycle. For a household making one or two drinks each morning rather than a run of lattes, that's not a real limitation — it's exactly the use case this machine is sized for.

The MARENZA jump: what a built-in grinder and LCD add for $329.99

The CASABREWS MARENZA Espresso Machine with Built-in Grinder and Steam Wand lists at $299.99 in our database, with a current listing price noted in our brief at $329.99 — the kind of $20–30 live-price movement that's normal on Amazon and exactly why we tell readers to confirm the price before buying rather than trust a snapshot. Either way, the MARENZA sits well above the rest of the lineup, and the reason is that it's solving a different problem than the other three machines.

The 3700 Essential and both CM5418 listings are machines you feed pre-ground coffee. The MARENZA has a grinder built in, which means the single most expensive add-on to any of the cheaper CASABREWS machines — a decent burr grinder, which typically runs $80 to $200 on its own — is already inside the box. It also adds an LCD display, which on a home espresso machine usually means visible brew settings and status rather than a touchscreen recipe system; we don't have a spec sheet breaking out exactly what the display shows, so we won't guess beyond "it displays machine status more legibly than an indicator light." The listing also specifies a 20-bar pump and describes the unit as PFAS-free, a materials claim about the brew path rather than a performance spec — we're repeating it because it's in the product listing, not vouching for it independently.

The MARENZA's size/variant is listed at 15.04 × 11.81 × 8.9 inches, which is a markedly different footprint than the 3700 Essential's 12.6 × 5.7 × 12.2 inches — wider and deeper, shorter. That shape is typical of a machine that has to fit a bean hopper and grinder mechanism above the brew group, and it means the MARENZA needs more counter width than the 3700 Essential needs counter depth. Measure before you buy, especially if the machine is going under a cabinet.

Whether the jump from $149.99 (the pricier CM5418) to $299.99–$329.99 (the MARENZA) is worth it comes down to one question: do you already own a grinder? If yes, the price gap buys you an LCD and a 20-bar pump rating on a machine that otherwise competes with the CM5418 on size. If no, the MARENZA is replacing two purchases — a $170 machine and an $80–200 grinder — with one, and on that math it's frequently the cheaper route to a complete setup, not the more expensive one.

The trade-off is buying a grinder separately, or committing to pre-ground coffee, which caps how good the shot can be.

Best if you want a slightly bigger machine: either CM5418

Both CM5418 listings include a steam wand and sit between the 3700 Essential and the MARENZA on price. Since our verified data shows no documented feature gap between the $139.99 and $149.99 listings, we'd default to the cheaper one unless the specific listing you're viewing clearly states an extra you want.

Best all-in-one: the MARENZA

If you don't already own a burr grinder, the MARENZA's $299.99–$329.99 price is doing the work of two purchases at once. It's the only machine in this lineup built around that integration, and the only one with an LCD display.

Steam wand consistency across the lineup, honestly

All four current CASABREWS machines in this comparison ship with a steam wand rather than an automatic one-touch frother — that's consistent across the 3700 Essential, both CM5418 listings and the MARENZA, and it's a real point in the brand's favor if you want control over milk texture rather than a fixed automatic setting. A manual wand means you decide how much air to introduce and for how long, which is also how you get genuine microfoam for a flat white rather than the looser foam an automatic frother tends to produce.

What we can't do honestly is rank the four wands against each other on performance, because our product data doesn't include wand-specific specs like steam pressure or heat-up time per model, and we have no verified owner-review data to draw on for any of these four listings — this site doesn't publish paraphrased review sentiment, because we have no way to confirm it's representative rather than cherry-picked. What we can say from the size and price tier these machines occupy: compact single-boiler machines under $200, which covers the 3700 Essential and both CM5418 listings, typically require a short wait between pulling a shot and getting the boiler to steam temperature, because one heating element is doing both jobs in sequence rather than two elements working in parallel. The MARENZA's larger footprint and higher price point don't come with a published dual-boiler spec in our data, so we're not going to claim it solves that wait — only that its price class is one where that's a more common feature to look for and confirm on the specific listing.

If steam-wand consistency across back-to-back drinks is the deciding factor for your household — say, two lattes made within a few minutes of each other most mornings — that's a spec worth confirming directly on whichever listing you're about to buy, rather than assuming it scales with price inside this lineup.

Which CASABREWS model actually matches a one-or-two-cup morning

Most home espresso mornings are one or two cups, not a run of drinks for a houseful of guests, and that changes which machine in this lineup is the right fit. A single small boiler recovering between shots is a non-issue if there's only one or two shots to pull. Counter space and whether you already own a grinder end up mattering more than steam-wand throughput.

On that basis: the 3700 Essential is sized and priced exactly for a one-or-two-cup morning where you already have a grinder, or are happy with pre-ground coffee — its 5.7-inch width is the smallest in the lineup, which matters more on a tight counter than any spec difference between the CM5418 listings. A CM5418 makes sense if you want a touch more machine for daily use without committing to the MARENZA's price or footprint, keeping in mind the two listings appear to be the same hardware at two prices based on what we can verify. The MARENZA is the pick if a one-or-two-cup morning is also the only coffee routine in the house and you'd rather not own a separate grinder at all — the built-in grinder means the entire routine, bean to cup, lives in one machine with one footprint, which for a single-person or two-person household is often the simpler choice even at the higher price.
