Why hopper capacity is a refill-frequency question, not a quality one

It's tempting to read a bigger bean hopper as a better machine, the same way a bigger water tank reads as more convenient. But a hopper doesn't touch the shot the way the grinder, the pump or the boiler do. All it does is decide how often you have to stop and pour more beans in — nothing about its size makes the coffee that comes out of it taste better or worse.

If anything, the relationship can run the other way. Whole beans hold their flavor far longer than ground coffee does, but they don't hold it forever, and a hopper that's rarely emptied is a hopper that's rarely refreshed. A smaller hopper you empty and refill every day or two is arguably the better habit for flavor, even though it reads as the less impressive spec on a listing page.

That's the frame for the four machines below. None of them publish identical information: one gives a gram number, one gives a shot count, and two describe their grinders' adjustability instead of saying anything about hopper size at all. Reading those differences correctly matters more than ranking them by a single number — because there isn't a single number to rank them by.

EUHOMY's stated 200g hopper: what that converts to in shots per fill

The EUHOMY Automatic Espresso Machine with Built-in Grinder and Milk Frother ($339.99, recorded November 9, 2026) is the one machine here that states its bean hopper capacity as a hard number: 200 grams. That's useful precisely because it's rare — most listings in this category describe the grinder, the pressure, the milk system, and leave hopper size as a dimension you'd have to measure yourself.

200 grams only means something once you convert it into shots, and that conversion has to state its assumption: a standard double-shot dose is around 18 grams of dry coffee. On that basis, a 200-gram hopper holds roughly eleven doubles before it needs a refill — 200 divided by 18, rounded down. If your habit is lighter, a single-shot dose closer to 9 grams would stretch the same 200 grams to roughly twenty-two singles.

Turn that into days rather than doses and the number gets smaller fast. At two doubles a day, eleven doubles lasts about five to six days. At one double a day, it stretches closer to a week and a half. Either way, a 200-gram hopper is not a "fill it once a month" proposition for a typical one- or two-person household — it's a roughly weekly refill, which is worth knowing before you assume "visible hopper" means "rarely open the bag."

Reemix's fixed hopper: sized for exactly two shots, nothing else

The Reemix 6 Bar Espresso Machine with Built-in Conical Burr Grinder ($79.99, recorded October 8, 2026) takes the opposite approach to describing its hopper. There's no gram figure published for it at all. Instead, the listing describes the hopper by function: it's built to hold enough whole beans for exactly two shots at a time, on a fixed grind setting rather than an adjustable one.

That's a meaningfully different design brief than EUHOMY's. EUHOMY's hopper is a small reservoir you refill on a cycle of several days. Reemix's hopper isn't a reservoir in that sense — it's portioned for the session in front of you. You load beans for the shots you're about to pull, not for the week.

At $79.99, that's also the cheapest machine in this comparison, and the simplicity of a fixed grind setting plus a two-shot hopper tracks with that price: fewer adjustable parts, less to calibrate, and a workflow that's closer to "load, grind, brew" than to "set it up once and top it off for days." If you want to dial grind size yourself, or if you want a hopper that holds more than one sitting's worth of beans, that's exactly what the two machines in the next section add — at a higher price.

HERTELLA and Canoly: two 44-setting adjustable grinders, both removable for cleaning

Between EUHOMY's gram figure and Reemix's two-shot design sit two machines that answer the capacity question a third way: they don't state a hopper size at all, and instead spec the grinder's adjustability. The HERTELLA Espresso Machine with Burr Grinder ($199.00) and the Canoly Fast Cold Brew Espresso Machine with Grinder ($239.99, recorded November 2, 2026) both carry a 44-setting burr grinder with a hopper that's removable for cleaning.

Neither listing publishes a gram or shot-count figure for how much the hopper itself holds, so we're not going to invent one — if a specific capacity number matters to your decision, check the current listing for either machine before buying. What both do specify clearly is grind adjustability and a hopper you can take off rather than one fixed in place, which is a different kind of commitment than a capacity number: it's a promise about control and maintenance, not about how long a fill lasts.

The two machines aren't identical beyond that shared spec. HERTELLA adds a 20-bar visual pressure gauge, a milk frother steam wand, a stainless-steel body and four temperature modes that include both hot and cold brew, plus an automatic pressure release function. Canoly adds its own fast cold-brew function on top of standard espresso, lattes and cappuccinos, along with a barista kit and a capsule handle. If cold brew specifically is the draw, both machines offer a version of it; Canoly's listing leads with it, while HERTELLA folds it into a broader set of temperature modes.

Why a removable hopper matters more than its size for oily dark-roast beans

Capacity tells you how often you refill. It doesn't tell you anything about what's left behind inside the hopper and the grind chamber between refills — and that's where a removable hopper earns its keep in a way raw gram figures don't.

Dark-roast beans are oilier than light or medium roasts, because longer roasting drives more of the bean's internal oils to the surface. Those oils don't stay neatly on the beans; they transfer to whatever the beans touch, including the inside walls of a hopper and the chute that feeds the grinder. Over weeks, that residue builds up into a thin, sticky film — the kind of thing that eventually turns rancid and taints the next batch of beans that passes through it, regardless of how fresh those new beans were when you poured them in.

A hopper fixed permanently to the machine can only be cleaned around its edges — wiped where you can reach, left alone where you can't. A hopper that comes off, like the ones on the HERTELLA and the Canoly, can be pulled free and washed properly, oil film and all, the same way you'd wash any other kitchen container that held something greasy. That's a maintenance advantage that has nothing to do with how many grams the hopper holds; a small removable hopper you actually clean beats a larger fixed one you can't.

We don't have a stated removable-hopper spec for the EUHOMY or the Reemix, so we're not asserting one either way — if dark roast is your daily bean and a washable hopper matters to you, that's a question for the current listing or the manual before you buy either of those two.

How much hopper space a daily one-or-two-cup habit actually needs

Run the numbers for an ordinary home routine rather than a worst case. One double a day at 18 grams is 18 grams of beans consumed daily; two doubles a day is 36 grams. Over a week, that's 126 grams for a one-double habit, or 252 grams for a two-double habit — both assuming an 18g dose, the same assumption used for the EUHOMY conversion above.

Compare that to EUHOMY's 200-gram hopper: it covers a one-double-a-day household for a bit over a week, and a two-double-a-day household for closer to five or six days. Either way, you're opening a fresh bag of beans at least weekly no matter which of these four machines you buy — a 200-gram hopper doesn't eliminate that errand, it just sets its rhythm.

Seen from that angle, Reemix's two-shot hopper isn't really the compromise it looks like on paper. If your household drinks one or two doubles a day, you're refilling a bean container on a weekly basis regardless of which machine you own — the only real difference is whether you're topping off a reservoir every few days or loading exactly what you need right before you brew. The two-shot design just makes that refill explicit and immediate instead of hiding it behind a number that still runs out within the week. Where a bigger stated capacity actually changes the routine is a household pulling three or more shots a day, or one serving multiple people back to back — at that volume, EUHOMY's 200 grams buys real breathing room that a two-shot hopper can't.

Frequently asked questions

How many shots does a 200-gram bean hopper actually hold?

Assuming a standard 18-gram double-shot dose, 200 grams works out to roughly eleven doubles before a refill. At a lighter 9-gram single-shot dose, the same 200 grams stretches to about twenty-two singles. That's the EUHOMY's stated hopper capacity; the other three machines in this comparison don't publish a gram figure for their hoppers.

Does a bigger hopper mean fresher coffee?

Not necessarily — if anything it can work the other way. Whole beans hold their flavor longer than ground coffee, but a hopper that's rarely emptied is a hopper that's rarely refreshed with new beans. A smaller hopper refilled every day or two can mean fresher coffee in the chamber than a large one topped off only once a week.

Why don't all of these machines list a hopper capacity in grams?

Because listings describe capacity in whichever way the manufacturer chose to communicate it. EUHOMY states a gram figure. Reemix describes its hopper by shot count instead — sized for exactly two shots. HERTELLA and Canoly skip a capacity figure entirely and instead specify their grinders' 44 adjustable settings and a hopper that's removable for cleaning. None of those approaches is more or less accurate; they're just answering different questions.

Does a removable hopper need to come off every time I clean the machine?

Not every time — but removability matters most with oily dark-roast beans, which leave a residue on hopper walls and the grind chute that builds up over weeks and can eventually turn rancid. A removable hopper, like the ones on the HERTELLA and the Canoly, lets you take it off periodically and wash it properly. A fixed hopper can only be wiped where you can reach it.
