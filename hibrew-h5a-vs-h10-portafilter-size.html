The short answer

Buy the HIBREW H5A at $113.99 if you want the cheapest real entry into pump espresso and you are not thinking past this one machine. Buy the HIBREW H10 at $169.99 if you want a basket size that matches the wider world of espresso gear — bigger baskets, more bottomless portafilters, more tampers sized to fit — and a pressure system you can adjust rather than accept as fixed.

The spec sheets look similar at a glance: both are semi-automatic, both have a steam wand, both come with a portafilter rather than a capsule bay. The $56 that separates them is concentrated almost entirely in two places — the portafilter's diameter, and what HIBREW built around the H10's group to let you tune pressure and release it with one touch. Neither machine beats the other outright. They answer different questions about how long you expect to keep buying accessories for whichever one you pick.

Why a portafilter's diameter outlives the machine

A portafilter basket is a fixed metal cylinder, and its diameter sets a hard ceiling on everything that touches it afterward: which baskets fit, which tampers seat flush against the rim, and which bottomless (naked) portafilters you can bolt onto the group later if you want to watch the extraction. Buy a tamper sized for the wrong basket and it either rocks in the rim or doesn't reach the edges, and an uneven tamp is one of the easiest ways to get a channeled, uneven shot.

58 millimeters is the long-standing commercial-café size — it's the diameter behind the espresso bar you've stood in front of, and it's the number most prosumer and commercial machines outside the ultra-compact category are built around. A machine with a 58mm group sits inside a much larger accessory ecosystem: more third-party baskets in more hole patterns, more bottomless portafilters, more 58mm-specific tampers and leveling tools, because 58mm is what most of that gear is made to fit.

51 millimeters is a smaller, less common basket size, typically found on compact single-serve-style semi-automatics like the H5A. It is not a defective size — espresso brews the same way at 9 bar regardless of basket diameter — but it narrows your aftermarket options. Fewer brands bother machining tampers, baskets or bottomless portafilters to fit 51mm, because fewer machines use it. You are more likely to end up buying from HIBREW directly, or hunting generic "fits 51mm" listings, than browsing a wide shelf of options the way a 58mm owner can.

Basket diameter has a second, quieter effect: for the same dose of ground coffee, a narrower basket holds a taller, denser puck than a wider one does. That's simple geometry, not a product claim about either machine — the same 18 or so grams has less room to spread sideways in a 51mm ring than in a 58mm one. A taller puck isn't automatically worse, but it does mean the water has slightly more depth to travel through evenly, which is one more reason dialing in grind size carefully matters a little more on the smaller basket.

Neither size changes what a correctly dialed-in shot tastes like. What changes is how easy it is to keep upgrading pieces around the machine without upgrading the machine itself — the same logic we walk through for a different basket size in our roundup of 58mm machines under $300.

What $113.99 gets you: the HIBREW H5A

The H5A is a 15-bar stainless steel semi-automatic with a 51mm portafilter and a scoop included for measuring and tamping cappuccino-style drinks. It adds a temperature display, so you can see where the boiler sits rather than guess from a light, a hot water function for Americanos or tea, and an adjustable steam wand for texturing milk by hand.

That is a real, usable entry setup for $113.99: a pump pulling genuine espresso pressure, a portafilter you can dial in rather than a sealed pod bay, and milk capability without a separate frother. The 15-bar figure on the box is the pump's maximum rating rather than the pressure at the coffee — espresso brews at roughly 9 bar at the puck — but every pump machine in this comparison, including the H10, reaches that regardless of what number is printed on it.

What the H5A does not include, because HIBREW doesn't list it: a stated pump-bar figure beyond that 15-bar rating, a tank-size spec, or a wattage figure. Where we don't have a published number, we're not going to guess at one — check the current Amazon listing for anything beyond what's above.

The included scoop is a small detail worth noting on its own, because it's one of the few accessories HIBREW bundles rather than leaves to the aftermarket. A scoop sized to the 51mm basket means you have a day-one way to measure and level a dose without separately sourcing a dosing tool — useful precisely because, as the next section covers, dosing and leveling tools made specifically for 51mm are harder to find later than ones made for 58mm.

What the extra $56 buys: the HIBREW H10

The H10's listing leads with two things, and both sit at the group head rather than anywhere else on the machine: a customizable 58mm group and one-touch pressure release. The 58mm basket is the headline upgrade over the H5A's 51mm — it's the size most of the wider espresso accessory market is built around, which matters more the longer you own the machine than it does on day one.

"Customizable pressure" means the H10 doesn't hold you to a single fixed brew pressure the way a simpler pump machine does — you get a degree of control over the shot rather than one preset outcome. One-touch pressure release addresses the moment every portafilter owner eventually fights: twisting a pressurized portafilter out of the group right after a shot, with hot water and grounds looking for somewhere to go. A dedicated release step is meant to relieve that pressure on command, rather than leaving you to wait it out or wrestle the handle.

Beyond the group and the release mechanism, HIBREW's listing for the H10 doesn't spell out a pump-bar rating, wattage, or tank size the way the H5A's does — so we're not assigning it a number it hasn't published. If a steam wand or boiler spec matters to your decision, that's a question for the current listing, not for us to estimate.

It's also worth being precise about what "customizable" is not claiming. It isn't a programmable-recipe system like the preset buttons on a super-automatic, and it isn't the same thing as PID temperature control, which regulates heat rather than pressure. It's a pressure-side adjustment sitting on top of a 58mm group — a more involved feature than a one-touch coffee button, and one that rewards the same kind of attention you'd put into grind size on either machine.

The picks

Pick the H5A if $113.99 is closer to your ceiling, you want a temperature display and a hot water tap you can actually use, and you're buying this to find out whether espresso at home is a habit worth keeping — not to build a long-term accessory collection around it.

Pick the H10 if $169.99 is comfortably inside your budget, you want the group size that matches the broader market of baskets and bottomless portafilters, and the idea of adjusting pressure and releasing it with one touch is worth $56 to you on its own. It is the more future-proof basket size, not necessarily the better cup out of the box — the two machines share the same basic pressure-brewing physics.

Neither is a bad buy at its price. The decision is really about which constraint bothers you more: spending less now, or being boxed into 51mm-specific gear later.

Aftermarket accessories, at each size

This is the part of the decision that doesn't show up until months after the purchase, when the stock single basket starts to feel limiting and you go looking for an upgrade.

On the H10's 58mm side, you're shopping in the same aisle as a large share of the prosumer and commercial espresso market. Precision baskets with more consistent hole patterns, bottomless portafilters that let you watch the puck extract, and tampers machined to a snug 58mm fit are all comparatively easy to find from third-party brands that never made a HIBREW machine. That depth of options is the practical payoff of the size, more than any single spec on the H10's own listing.

On the H5A's 51mm side, the aftermarket is thinner. 51mm isn't a size most independent accessory makers bother tooling for, since fewer machines on the market use it. That tends to mean sticking closer to what HIBREW sells directly, or searching generic "51mm" listings and checking fitment carefully before buying — a less certain process than picking a 58mm part off a shelf built for the size.

Neither basket size is an obstacle to making decent espresso with the machine as it ships. The difference only matters if you know you'll want to swap baskets, add a bottomless portafilter, or buy a better tamper somewhere down the line — in which case the H10's size removes a search problem the H5A's doesn't.

There's also a timing difference worth flagging. Shopping for 58mm parts, you can generally compare several brands side by side and expect consistent fitment, because so much of the market is built to that one number. Shopping for 51mm parts, fitment is the thing to double-check before buying rather than after — "fits most 51mm portafilters" on a generic listing is a looser promise than a part machined to a single, dominant standard. Read the listing's compatibility notes closely, and when in doubt, buy from HIBREW's own accessory line for the H5A rather than a generic third party.

Is the bigger basket worth it for one or two cups a day?

For a household pulling one or two shots in a morning, basket size by itself changes very little about the cup. A correctly dosed, correctly tamped shot in a 51mm basket and one in a 58mm basket both brew at the same roughly 9-bar pressure, and both can taste equally good or equally bad depending on grind and dose — the size of the basket isn't what decides that.

What a low-volume household is really deciding between is the other two things that come with the H10's $56: a customizable-pressure group and one-touch release, versus the H5A's temperature display and hot water tap. If you make one double shot and maybe an Americano each morning, the H5A's hot water function covers a need the H10's listing doesn't mention, and you may never bump into the aftermarket-accessories question at all, since you're not buying extra baskets for a machine you use lightly.

Where the H10 earns its $56 for a light household is the same place it earns it for a heavy one: optionality. If there's a real chance you'll want to swap in a better basket, try a bottomless portafilter, or simply like having pressure control even at low volume, the 58mm group buys you that door without requiring you to use it today. If you're confident you'll run the machine exactly as it ships and never touch the accessory question, the H5A's lower price and included hot water function are the more honest fit for a one-or-two-cup routine.

One thing not to assume

A bigger basket number does not mean a stronger pump or a hotter boiler. 58mm versus 51mm is a diameter, not a pressure rating — and neither HIBREW listing states a pump-bar figure for the H10 the way the H5A's 15-bar rating is stated for the H5A. Don't read "58mm" as shorthand for "more powerful"; it's a fitment spec, and the power specs that are actually published are the ones in the table above.

Frequently asked questions

Does a 58mm basket make better espresso than a 51mm basket?

Not by itself. Both sizes brew at the same roughly 9-bar pressure, and shot quality comes from grind size, dose and tamp rather than basket diameter. The practical difference is downstream: 58mm has a much larger market of third-party baskets, tampers and bottomless portafilters built to fit it, while 51mm options are comparatively limited.

What does "customizable pressure" mean on the HIBREW H10?

It means the H10's group is built to let you adjust brew pressure rather than lock you into one fixed outcome the way a simpler pump machine does. HIBREW's listing doesn't publish a specific pressure range for this feature, so beyond that general description, check the current listing for exact figures.

What does one-touch pressure release actually do?

It's meant to relieve pressure in the group on command after a shot, rather than making you wait for it to drop on its own before removing the portafilter — the moment that otherwise risks hot water and spent grounds escaping as you twist the handle out.

Is the HIBREW H10 worth $56 more than the H5A for a household making one or two cups a day?

It depends on what you value more: the H5A's temperature display and hot water function cover a light daily routine well and cost less, while the H10's 58mm basket and pressure control mainly pay off if you expect to want better accessories or more control later. At low volume, basket size alone won't change your cup — it changes your options down the road.
